# Context and sources

Draft for author review · sources checked 14 September 2026

## Why this question matters

TikTok's moderation decisions sit within a wider debate about child safety, automated systems and the people who perform moderation. This project uses public reporting to establish that context, then examines a narrower empirical question: how reported decision counts and automation shares vary, particularly in the protection-of-minors category.

## What the analysis can contribute

The monthly analysis identifies a pronounced increase in fully automated protection-of-minors decisions in March and April 2024. This predates the reported Berlin dispute in 2025 and must not be attributed to those later layoffs. The matched January–May comparison across 2024, 2025 and 2026 asks how reported volumes and shares changed; it does not identify who performed the work or estimate the effect of a staffing decision.

A growing non-automated share does not necessarily mean more non-automated decisions. Counts and shares are therefore presented together. The observations may motivate further research into reporting practices, moderation actions and organisational changes, but do not establish a causal connection between them.

## Evidence boundaries and open questions

- The public records used here do not identify the Berlin team's contribution to TikTok-wide counts, its replacement contractor, or an exact operational handover date.
- The sources above distinguish the union's position, independent reporting and the company's general transparency statement. A direct company response to the specific Berlin allegations has not been established in this note.
- Internal promotion promises, individual employment experiences and recollections are not included as verified public evidence.
- Announcements, dismissal notices and final employment dates are different events. The November report should not be treated as proof that every employee left on that date.
- This is a selected contextual timeline, not a comprehensive history or an update on the final outcome of all regulatory proceedings.

## Data source

The analysis uses downloaded aggregates from the [European Commission's DSA Transparency Database](https://transparency.dsa.ec.europa.eu/). Source documentation: [database documentation](https://transparency.dsa.ec.europa.eu/page/documentation). The repository README describes the analysis scope and coverage limitations. News and institutional statements provide context; they are not input rows in the statistical analysis.
